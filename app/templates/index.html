1. Target Definition & CPCB Methodology

The target is the CPCB-method-derived citywide daily AQI estimate. Individual station hourly sub-indices are calculated using piecewise linear interpolation across standard Indian CPCB breakpoints for 7 criteria pollutants (PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, CO, Ozone, NH3), requiring at least 3 pollutants with one being either PM2.5 or PM10. Daily mean is derived from eligible hourly averages across 37 stations in Delhi.

2. Out-of-Time Chronological Split (No Data Leakage)

Random k-fold cross-validation is invalid for time series due to look-ahead data leakage. We implemented a strict chronological split:
• Train Set: 2024-01-01 to 2024-12-31 (365 days)
• Validation Set: 2025-01-01 to 2025-06-30 (181 days for model selection)
• Final Test Set: 2025-07-01 to 2025-12-31 (184 held-out days)

3. Why Day 1 Beats Baseline & Day 3 Uses Fallback

• 1-Day Ahead: GradientBoosting achieved MAE 15.78 vs Persistence MAE 21.50 (a 26.6% error reduction). The regressor effectively uses 7-day rolling mean, cyclic seasonal encoding (sin/cos of day-of-year), and meteorology.
• 2-Day Ahead: RandomForest achieved MAE 29.03 vs Persistence MAE 30.72. Marginal improvement.
• 3-Day Ahead: HistGradientBoosting scored MAE 39.10, losing to Persistence MAE 33.43! Instead of deploying a worse model, we established an honest scientific fallback policy: persistence baseline is used for horizon 3.

4. Why Live 2026 Predictions Show "Unavailable"

The model requires 7 days of continuous ground-station derived AQI lags and station-averaged pollutant features up to the forecast origin. The offline archive ends 2025-12-31. Substituting Open-Meteo European AQI or US AQI is scientifically invalid because their sub-index formulas, breakpoints, and sensor sources differ radically from Indian CPCB standards. Reporting "Unavailable" is the honest engineering choice.
